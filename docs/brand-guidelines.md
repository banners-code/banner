## Positioning

Kinetelle is a live visual instrument for creators. It gives people a professional space to bring a camera feed into view, describe a direction, and explore how a moment could look.

**Promise:** Shape the moment.

**Product descriptor:** A live canvas for visual transformation.

## Personality and voice

- **Assured:** speak clearly about the craft without boasting.
- **Artful:** use language about light, texture, framing, and movement.
- **Composed:** keep controls and copy calm, direct, and useful.
- **Curious:** encourage exploration without implying every result is predictable.

Use concise verbs such as *shape*, *restyle*, *frame*, and *explore*. Avoid hype, fake urgency, and claims that the demo is already powered by AI.

## Visual identity

The identity pairs an ink-dark studio environment with warm mineral surfaces and a small signal-lime accent. Large, editorial display type gives the marketing page a distinct voice; compact monospaced labels make the Studio feel like a considered creative instrument.

### Typography

- **Display:** Instrument Serif, with a system serif fallback. Use for large marketing statements and selected editorial moments.
- **Interface:** DM Sans, with a system sans fallback. Use for navigation, controls, and body copy.
- **Metadata:** DM Mono, with a system monospace fallback. Use sparingly for statuses, indices, and technical labels.

### Mark and imagery

The wordmark uses a quiet, custom drawn aperture mark: two offset rounded frames that suggest a lens opening and a changing frame. Use the mark with the Kinetelle wordmark; keep it lime on dark surfaces or ink on chalk. Do not use emojis as interface icons.

Imagery should feel like a moving portrait or a deliberate light study, with deep shadows, tactile grain, and restrained color. Demo UI must label simulated output as a demonstration.

## Interface principles

1. Keep the transformed frame as the visual center of the Studio.
2. Keep inputs and editing controls close to the preview they affect.
3. Show permission, camera, and demo states in plain language.
4. Make every control keyboard accessible with visible focus.
5. Use motion to clarify state changes; respect reduced-motion preferences.
6. Preserve a focused vertical workflow on narrow screens.
